# Historical method

The Armoury distinguishes visible model features, documented terminology and historical comparisons.

## Model observations

Labels identify visible surface regions. They do not establish material composition, date, manufacture or authenticity. Surface textures are not material analyses.

## Historical comparisons

Museum records are cited for specific objects and terms. Dates stay attached to those objects.

The Roman Hod Hill comparison has a complex relationship between its blade and fittings; it is not proof of a single standardized legionary sword. The Anglo-Saxon T-axe is discussed as a woodworking tool. Japanese blade dates and mounting dates can differ.

## Construction and separation

The geometry preserves source components or creates educational planar divisions of merged surfaces. Neither method reveals absent interiors. A separate digital cap is not evidence of a modeled tang or historical peening. Construction texts describe documented methods, not an inspection of concealed model parts.

## Images and reading

Archive captions identify the source, date, institution, image license and relationship to the collection. Fight books are instructional images. The Algerian nimcha record distinguishes the dates and origins of its fittings from those of its blade. The pollaxe in Fiore is distinct from the woodworking T-axe.

The reading room links catalogues, technical articles and archaeological studies. It does not redistribute whole books. A source link supports the stated comparison; it does not authenticate the digital model.
